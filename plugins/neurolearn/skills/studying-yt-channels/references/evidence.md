# Evidence, review, and later questions

## Trace each material conclusion

Keep the chain: final conclusion -> source claim record -> retained transcript version and cue interval -> original video URL. Visually dependent conclusions also point to retained frames/crops and the observation method.

Use in-text links to the relevant video time, an explicit start–end interval, and short quotations supporting the formulation. Preserve the exact quotation in the cited transcript version. If interpreting several sources, cite the premises from those sources and identify the result as synthesis. Distinguish the speaker's assertion, an individual example, the agent's inference, and independently verified facts.

Read enough neighboring context to preserve speaker, question, negation, qualifications, uncertainty, audience, date, conditions, and later exceptions. Do not silently improve ambiguous ASR into a plausible quotation. Without audio verification, describe it as transcript-derived where that distinction matters.

## Independent review

The primary analyst reads every included video's complete received transcript. Another context reviews all material final conclusions and quotations, practically significant recommendations, quantities, limitations, causal/risk assertions, disputed interpretations, and flagged ambiguities.

For other intermediate records, use a declared sample across videos, analysts, topics, acquisition methods, and visual dependence. Select some passages directly from the original transcript independently of the analyst's claim list, so missing ideas can be found. Report exactly what was reviewed; a sample does not establish whole-corpus independent semantic completeness.

For assigned source-review material, first let the reviewer read the original and identify important thoughts and qualifications without the analyst's proposed verdict. Then compare with the saved analysis. For checking a particular final claim, give its text and original evidence but not the expected verdict or the author's rationale.

Save findings separately; the owning analyst repairs its record, and the reviewer rechecks the repair. A discovered defect expands the affected error class/worker review and invalidates dependent synthesis until repaired or explicitly qualified.

Deterministic checks apply to every completed record: file and version identity, source IDs, duplicate detection, interval validity, quote lookup within the asserted evidence, reference resolution, cue accounting, and completion state. They establish structural integrity, not semantic completeness or factual correctness of the speaker.

## Query an existing Knowledge Base

1. Read its policy, scope, state, topic index, and unresolved-issue register. Identify the source/version of the relevant entries. In a legacy base, use the owning manifests and original metadata; explicitly note missing policy/review records where material, rather than inventing them or starting a migration. A missing auxiliary index does not prevent a bounded answer supported by preserved original evidence.
2. Retrieve detailed source records and the original transcript intervals with neighboring context. Follow supplemental corrections/review findings; do not answer from a summary alone.
3. Answer the user's question directly, with source links, intervals, and supporting quotations. Keep context-specific advice context-specific and preserve contradictory positions when unresolved.
4. For a movement or demonstration, link the original interval and give only important source cautions rather than a prose reconstruction of the action.
5. State a material gap if the evidence does not answer the question. Ask before expanding scope or starting new paid acquisition when the existing authorization does not cover it.

This is read-only consultation by default. It does not regenerate the catalog, rewrite source text, run cleanup, or launch the full study graph. Cited retained frames and source text should remain usable after temporary media cleanup; reopening the original full video depends on its later availability.
